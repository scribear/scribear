Sources and attribution for test_audio_files/speech/
====================================================

These are third-party speech recordings used only as transcription test
fixtures. Each entry gives the upstream source, its licensing/rights status,
and the exact commands used to derive the file committed here (16 kHz mono
16-bit PCM WAV — Granite Speech's native rate).


harvard_16k_mono.wav
--------------------
Content : A single speaker reading the "Harvard sentences", IEEE list 1
          ("The birch canoe slid on the smooth planks...", ~34 s).
Upstream: Open Speech Repository (developed by Telchemy).
          https://www.voiptroubleshooter.com/open_speech/american.html
          file: OSR_us_000_0010_8k.wav
Rights  : Freely usable. Per the Open Speech Repository site, the material is
          "freely available for use in VoIP testing, research, development,
          marketing and any other reasonable application" and may be copied,
          downloaded and modified provided the source is identified as the
          "Open Speech Repository". Attribution: Open Speech Repository.
          (The Harvard Sentences text itself is the IEEE 1969 standard list.)
Derived :
    curl -sL -o osr.wav https://www.voiptroubleshooter.com/open_speech/american/OSR_us_000_0010_8k.wav
    ffmpeg -y -i osr.wav -ac 1 -ar 16000 -acodec pcm_s16le harvard_16k_mono.wav


apollo11_dialogue_16k_mono.wav
------------------------------
Content : Multi-speaker Apollo 11 mission dialogue — crew (Armstrong, Aldrin,
          Collins) and Houston CAPCOM, conversational turn-taking (~50 s).
          Chosen for a multi-person / multi-speaker transcription test.
Upstream: https://archive.org/details/Apollo11AudioHighlights
          file: Apollo11Highlights.mp3  (clip starting at 08:00 / 480 s, 50 s long)
          Digitized, cataloged and archived by the Houston Audio Control Room
          at the NASA Johnson Space Center; hosted on the Internet Archive.
Rights  : PUBLIC DOMAIN. A work of the U.S. Federal Government (NASA); such
          works are not eligible for copyright protection in the United States
          (17 U.S.C. Sec. 105).
Derived :
    ffmpeg -y -ss 480 -t 50 \
        -i "https://archive.org/download/Apollo11AudioHighlights/Apollo11Highlights.mp3" \
        -ac 1 -ar 16000 -acodec pcm_s16le apollo11_dialogue_16k_mono.wav
